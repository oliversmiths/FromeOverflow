Overflow Watch Frome

+ Legend

    Overflow Watch is a monitor of storm overflows on the River Frome and its tributaries around Frome, Somerset. It checks Wessex Water’s near real-time event duration monitor feed every 15 minutes and keeps its own history. It also displays Environment Agency Annual Return data.

    This project is not affiliated with Wessex Water. It covers the Frome catchment only and does not try to replace the national trackers. The intention is to give a hyperlocal window onto current and historical sewage overflow data.

    How to use

    Reading the map

    Each pin is an overflow, coloured by its state at the last check: red discharging now, amber discharged within the last 48 hours, green not discharging, grey no signal. Purple pins mark popular swimming spots, with the latest water quality and river flow reading where there is one.

    Reading the timeline

    Each overflow monitor is a card. The 90-day status strip shows one bar a day: red where it discharged, amber for the 48 hours after, green when it was clear, dark grey where the monitor was offline, striped before watching began.

    Switch card views from 90-day to History to see the Environment Agency's own regulator-verified annual return, year by year since 2024 (the earliest year that can be matched to this monitor's identifier – see Sources). The two averages below are deliberately different figures: the Environment Agency's own long-term spill average (its published baseline, often running back further than 2024), and our calculated average duration across years on record – there's no equivalent long-term-average-duration figure published, so that one is ours, not theirs.

    Safety

    This site records when storm overflows discharge and displays Environment Agency Annual Returns. For most of the river it does not measure water quality, and it cannot tell you whether a stretch is safe to enter. Two spots – Farleigh Hungerford and Tellisford Weir – show the latest water quality sample and river flow reading, but sampling there is roughly weekly in season, so even that is a recent snapshot, not a live guarantee.

    A stretch showing no recent discharge is not therefore clean. Rivers carry bacteria from farmland, wildlife and road runoff at all times, and hazards like cold or fast water remain regardless of what the overflows are doing.

    If you swim, paddle or let a dog in the Frome, judge each spot on its own merits. For a bathing water’s official classification see the Environment Agency’s Swimfo; for live pollution alerts and overflows beyond this one town, see Surfers Against Sewage’s Sewage Map.

    What it can’t tell you

    A monitor records the start and end of an overflow – not how much was released, and not what was in it. A monitor can also trigger on debris or vegetation, so an activation indicates a discharge rather than confirming one.

    An incomplete picture

    If a monitor spills twice between two checks, only the second is recorded, so the totals here are a floor, not an exact figure. They won’t match the Environment Agency’s regulator-verified annual return, shown for comparison in each monitor’s map popup – that figure is counted differently (and published roughly a year in arrears), not more up to date.
