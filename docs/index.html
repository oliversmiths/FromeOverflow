Overflow Watch Frome

    What this is

    Overflow Watch is a single-town monitor of storm overflows on the River Frome and its tributaries around Frome, Somerset. It checks Wessex Water’s near real-time event duration monitor feed every 15 minutes and keeps its own history.

    The Overflow Watch is not affiliated with Wessex Water. It covers the Frome catchment only and does not try to replace the national trackers.

    Reading the map

    Each pin is an overflow, coloured by its state at the last check: red discharging now, amber discharged within the last 48 hours, green not discharging, grey no signal.

    Reading the timeline

    Each overflow monitor is represented by a card, ranked by total discharge time. The 90-day status strip shows one bar a day: red where it discharged, amber for the 48 hours after, green when it was clear, dark grey where the monitor was offline, grey-hatched before watching began.

    River Frome

    Monitoring storm overflows is only one aspect of managing the health of the river Frome. For more information and to get involved please visit Friends of the River Frome.

    Safety

    This site records when storm overflows discharge. It does not measure water quality, and it cannot tell you whether a river is safe to enter.

    A stretch showing no recent discharge is not therefore clean. Rivers carry bacteria from farmland, wildlife and road runoff at all times, and hazards like cold or fast water remain regardless of what the overflows are doing.

    If you swim, paddle or let a dog in the Frome, judge each spot on its own merits. For a bathing water’s official classification see the Environment Agency’s Swimfo; for live pollution alerts and overflows beyond this one town, see Surfers Against Sewage’s Sewage Map.

    What it can’t tell you

    A monitor records the start and end of an overflow – not how much was released, and not what was in it. A monitor can also trigger on debris or vegetation, so an activation indicates a discharge rather than confirming one.

    An incomplete picture

    If a monitor spills twice between two checks, only the second is recorded, so the totals here are a floor, not an exact figure. These numbers will not match Wessex Water’s regulator-verified annual returns, which are counted differently.

    Data

    Overflow activity comes from Wessex Water’s near real-time event duration monitoring (EDM) feed – an ArcGIS service published to Water UK’s National Storm Overflow Hub. It reports only each monitor’s current status.

    Wessex Water Storm Overflow Activity © Wessex Water, licensed CC BY 4.0, used here filtered to the Frome catchment and rebuilt into a day-by-day record.

    Site names, waterbody, overflow type, treatment and cause come from a second Wessex layer, overflow context. Waterbody names are the Environment Agency’s Water Framework Directive catchments – look one up in the Catchment Data Explorer. No Environment Agency data is used directly, and nothing here is regulator-verified.
