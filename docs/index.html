Overflow Watch Frome

    Overflow Watch is a single-town monitor of storm overflows on the River Frome and its tributaries around Frome, Somerset. It checks Wessex Water’s near real-time event duration monitor feed every 15 minutes and keeps its own history.

    How to use

    Reading the map

    Each round pin is an overflow, coloured by its state at the last check: red discharging now, amber discharged within the last 48 hours, green not discharging, grey no signal. Blue diamonds mark popular swimming spots, with the latest water quality and river flow reading where there is one.

    Reading the timeline

    Each overflow monitor is represented by a card, ranked by total discharge time. The 90-day status strip shows one bar a day: red where it discharged, amber for the 48 hours after, green when it was clear, dark grey where the monitor was offline, striped before watching began.

    Safety

    This site records when storm overflows discharge. For most of the river it does not measure water quality, and it cannot tell you whether a stretch is safe to enter. Two spots – Farleigh Hungerford and Tellisford Weir – show the latest water quality sample and river flow reading in their map popups, but sampling there is roughly weekly in season, so even that is a recent snapshot, not a live guarantee.

    A stretch showing no recent discharge is not therefore clean. Rivers carry bacteria from farmland, wildlife and road runoff at all times, and hazards like cold or fast water remain regardless of what the overflows are doing.

    If you swim, paddle or let a dog in the Frome, judge each spot on its own merits. For a bathing water’s official classification see the Environment Agency’s Swimfo; for live pollution alerts and overflows beyond this one town, see Surfers Against Sewage’s Sewage Map.

    What it can’t tell you

    A monitor records the start and end of an overflow – not how much was released, and not what was in it. A monitor can also trigger on debris or vegetation, so an activation indicates a discharge rather than confirming one.

    An incomplete picture

    If a monitor spills twice between two checks, only the second is recorded, so the totals here are a floor, not an exact figure. They won’t match the Environment Agency’s regulator-verified annual return, shown for comparison in each monitor’s map popup – that figure is counted differently (and published roughly a year in arrears), not more up to date.

    Sources

    Data

    Overflow activity comes from Wessex Water’s near real-time event duration monitoring (EDM) feed, an ArcGIS service. It reports only each monitor’s current status, not history – this site polls it every 15 minutes and keeps its own day-by-day record.

    • Storm overflow activity – fetched from the live ArcGIS feed; browsable as Wessex Water Storm Overflow Activity on Water UK’s National Storm Overflow Hub. © Wessex Water, licensed CC BY 4.0. Filtered here to the Frome catchment and rebuilt into a day-by-day record.
    • Site metadata (site name, waterbody, overflow type, treatment, cause) – fetched from a second, separately licensed Wessex layer, overflow context.
    • Annual spill totals (the regulator-verified spill count and duration shown in each monitor’s map popup) – fetched from the Environment Agency’s EDM Storm Overflow Annual Return, browsable via their Storm Overflow Explorer. Contains Environment Agency data © Environment Agency copyright and/or database right, licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.
    • Swim spot water quality and river flow (shown in the Farleigh Hungerford and Tellisford Weir map popups) – fetched from a public ArcGIS FeatureServer behind Wessex Water’s Farleigh Hungerford Dashboard. Water quality sampling © Wessex Water; the river flow reading originates with the Environment Agency’s hydrology API.

    Waterbody names are Wessex’s own field, sourced from the Environment Agency’s Water Framework Directive catchment scheme.

    Map

    Streets and place names © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Rivers and streams come from two Ordnance Survey/Environment Agency sources instead – OSM’s own waterway tagging turned out to be thin for anything smaller than a named river near Frome.     River Frome

    The Overflow Watch is not affiliated with Wessex Water. It covers the Frome catchment only and does not try to replace the national trackers.

    Monitoring storm overflows is only one aspect of managing the health of the river Frome. For more information and to get involved please visit Friends of the River Frome and Frome Families for the Future.
